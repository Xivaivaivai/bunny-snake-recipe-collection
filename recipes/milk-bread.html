Milk Bread

Soft, pull-apart loaves shaped from rolled spirals, three to a pan.

Adapted from 做面包的Even’s milk bread tutorial on Xiaohongshu, then tested and adjusted in Bunny’s kitchen.

  • 2 loavesyield
  • ≈ 3½ hrstart to oven out
  • 325°Fbake
  • 6 × 170 gdough pieces

Loaf pan care

A little care keeps the pan browning evenly and releasing cleanly.

Before first use

Rinse the new pan with water and dry it thoroughly. Bake it empty at 350°F for 20–30 minutes. If you skip this, your first batch of loaves won't brown well.

After each bake

Wipe the pan clean with a damp, well-wrung paper towel. Try to avoid washing it whenever you can. If it gets messy, like after a chocolate loaf, wash it by hand and dry it thoroughly right away.

Never put the pan in the dishwasher.

Ingredients

2 loaves

Ingredient guide → which milk, eggs, butter, and yeast to use.

Mix

Everything goes in except butter and salt, then a cold rest before the real knead.

  1. Make a rough dough

    In the mixer bowl, combine the bread flour, milk, egg, sugar, milk powder, and yeast. Mix on low just until there's no dry flour left. It should look shaggy and rough. Keep the butter and salt aside for now.

  2. Chill and rest

    Cover the bowl and put it in the fridge. The flour hydrates while the dough rests, so gluten starts forming on its own, and the chill keeps the dough from getting too warm during the long knead that follows.

    30 min, in the fridge

  3. Knead in butter and salt

    Mix for about 1 minute to bring the chilled dough together. Add the softened butter and the salt, then knead on medium speed until smooth, glossy, and pulling away from the sides of the bowl.

    8–10 min kneading

  4. Check for windowpane

    Pinch off a small piece and gently stretch it between your fingers. When it stretches thin enough to see light through without tearing, the dough is ready. If it rips, knead another minute or two and test again.

Proof and shape

Each piece gets folded, rested, then rolled into a loose spiral.

  1. First proof

    Round the dough, place it in a lightly greased bowl, and cover. Let it rise somewhere warm (around 78–80°F, like an oven with just the light on) until clearly puffy and close to doubled. It's okay if it hasn't fully doubled. What matters most is not letting it over-proof.

    About 40 min

  2. Divide and rest

    Press out the air gently and divide the dough into 6 pieces of 165–170 g each. Round each into a ball, cover, and let them relax.

    Rest 10 min

  3. Flatten and fold in thirds

    Flatten each ball into a square about 5 inches across. Fold it in thirds like a letter: one side to the middle, then the other side over the top. Cover and rest again.

    Keep it gentle. Pat the ball out with your palm or fingertips instead of pressing it flat, and lay the folds over without stretching them. A few air bubbles are fine to leave in.
    Rest 10 min

  4. Roll out and roll up

    Turn each folded piece seam side up. Roll it out along its length into a long strip, about 10–12 inches, then roll it up from the short end into a snug spiral. Pinch the seam lightly. Set the roll seam side down, but don't tuck the seam all the way under or press the roll down onto it.

    Keep it gentle. Don't press hard with the rolling pin or squeeze the roll all the way down. Leaving a little air in the dough gives you a taller, softer loaf.

  5. Place in the pans

    Set 3 rolls in each greased loaf pan, seam side down, spirals all facing the same long side of the pan. Leave a little space between them to rise into.

    3 rolls per pan, 2 pans

Bake

  1. Final proof

    Cover and let rise in a warm spot until the dough fills about 80% of the pan. Timing depends on how warm your kitchen is.

    Usually 60–80 min

  2. Bake

    Bake at 325°F until golden and the center reaches 180°F on a thermometer. Pulling it at 180°F keeps the crumb soft and tender.

    23–25 min at 325°F

  3. Cool, slice, and pack

    Tap the pans firmly on the counter once to release steam, which helps keep the sides from caving in. Turn the loaves out right away onto a rack. They'll come out lying on their sides, so gently stand them upright right away. Let them cool until the crust is about skin temperature and still feels just slightly warm to the touch, then slice and pack.

    Cool until barely warm
