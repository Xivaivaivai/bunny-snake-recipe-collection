Red Bean Buns

Milk bread dough wrapped around a generous ball of chunky red bean paste, baked until soft and golden.

  • 14 bunsyield
  • 65–70 g + 45 gdough + filling
  • ≈ 3 hrstart to oven out
  • 360°Fbake 16–18 min

Ingredients

14 buns

Dough

Filling

Red bean pasteShirakiku, 500 g pack. Coarse paste gives more texture than fine, but either works, so go with what you like.

Topping

WaterFor misting the tops before bakinga spritz
Black or white sesameOptional, just for decorationa pinch
Melted butterOptional, brushed on after baking for a shiny finisha little

At 14 buns, this is one full batch of milk bread dough, cut into pieces of 65–70 g. One batch usually makes 13–14.

Ingredient guide → which milk, eggs, butter, and yeast to use.

Make the dough

The dough is exactly the milk bread dough, start to finish.

  1. Mix and knead

    Follow steps 1–4 of the milk bread recipe: rough dough, 30-minute chill, knead in the butter and salt, then check for a windowpane.

    About 50 min

  2. First proof

    Round the dough, place it in a lightly greased bowl, cover, and let it rise somewhere warm (around 78–80°F) until clearly puffy and close to doubled. As with the loaves, don’t let it over-proof.

    About 40 min

Fill and shape

Portion the filling while the dough proofs, so shaping goes quickly.

  1. Portion the filling

    Scoop the red bean paste into 14 balls of 45 g and roll each one smooth. Set them on a plate and chill until you’re ready to shape. If there’s paste left in the pack, roll it into 45 g balls too and freeze them, so they’re ready to go next time.

    Chilled filling wraps cleanly. Cold, firm balls are much easier to enclose than soft paste straight from the pack.

  2. Divide and rest

    Press out the air gently and divide the dough into 14 pieces of 65–70 g. Round each into a ball, cover, and let them relax.

    Rest 10 min

  3. Flatten and fill

    Pat each ball out gently into a round just a little larger than the filling ball. Don’t flatten it too much. Keep the center a little thicker than the edges, then set a ball of filling in the middle.

    Thick middle, thin edges. At 45 g of filling to 65 g of dough, the base needs to be sturdy so the paste doesn’t push through the bottom, while thin edges keep the seam from turning doughy.

  4. Seal

    Hold the bun in one hand. Using the web between your thumb and index finger, push the dough up around the filling, turning the bun as you go, until the edges meet at the top. Pinch them together firmly. Turn the bun seam side down and cup it gently to round it. Place on a parchment-lined tray, about 2 inches apart.

    Pinch it well. A seam that isn’t fully sealed tends to pop open in the oven and leak filling.

Bake

  1. Final proof

    Cover loosely and let the buns rise in a warm spot until puffy and about 1½ times their size. They should spring back slowly when pressed lightly. Preheat the oven to 360°F toward the end.

    Usually 40–50 min

  2. Mist and bake

    Lightly mist the tops with water. If you like, sprinkle a few sesame seeds in the center for decoration. Bake at 360°F until golden.

    Use a light-colored baking sheet. A dark pan browns the bottoms too fast. If your oven runs hot from the bottom (common in flat-bottom ovens with a strong lower element), stack two baking sheets to keep the bottoms from overbrowning.
    16–18 min at 360°F

  3. Cool and pack

    Move the buns to a rack right away. If you like a shiny finish, brush the tops with melted butter while they’re hot; skip it for a matte crust. Let them cool until just barely warm before packing, so steam doesn’t soften the tops.

    Cool until barely warm
